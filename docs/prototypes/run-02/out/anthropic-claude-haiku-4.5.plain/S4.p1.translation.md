**Q3. What does the tiny conclusion hand the reader so "keep, don't invest" is actionable?**

The ticket flags for kill and keep are easy to write and hard to act on. Since you want the conclusion small, it can't include an implementation plan — and implementing the verdict is outside the map's scope anyway. The question is what minimal payload turns the verdict into a *decision* rather than a shrug.

My recommendation: three short elements.

1. **Explicit non-actions** — what "don't invest" rules out: no new tooling, no format redesign, no recurring metrics pipeline (already out of scope per the map), and no kill.

2. **Revisit tripwires** — the conditions that reopen the question, so "keep" doesn't mean "never look again." Concretely: revisit if updates hit zero for a full quarter (the surface died on its own), or if the server materially revives (then "improve" becomes worth pricing).

3. **One cheap follow-up pointer** — the data can't see DMs, introductions, or offline value, but it names the small group who received most of the visible value. The conclusion notes that asking them directly is the cheap way to close the false-negative gap. A pointer only — actually doing it is a new effort, and the map ruled surveys out of this study.

|---------|--------------|
| Explicit non-actions | Blocks wrong moves |
| Revisit tripwires | Keeps the door open |
| Cheap follow-up pointer | Closes the gap you can't measure |
