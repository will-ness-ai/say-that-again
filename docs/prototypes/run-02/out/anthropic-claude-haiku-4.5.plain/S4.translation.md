Verdict locked, on my call: **keep, don't invest.** Kill misreads a server-wide collapse as a problem with its most resilient part, and that's the most dangerous mistake given what we can't measure. Improve spends effort on a supply problem no format change fixes. Keep costs nothing and keeps the door open if the server comes back.

**Q3. What does the tiny conclusion need so "keep, don't invest" is actionable?**

The ticket flags for kill and keep are easy to write and hard to act on. Since you want the conclusion small, it can't hold an implementation plan. Implementing the verdict is out of scope anyway. So the question is what minimal set of facts turns the verdict into a *decision* instead of a shrug.

My recommendation: three short elements.

1. **Explicit non-actions** — what "don't invest" rules out: no new tooling, no format redesign, no recurring metrics pipeline, and no kill.

2. **Revisit tripwires** — the conditions that reopen the question, so "keep" doesn't mean "never look again." Concretely: revisit if updates hit zero for a full quarter (the surface died on its own), or if the server materially revives (then "improve" becomes worth pricing).

3. **One cheap follow-up pointer** — the data can't see DMs, introductions, or offline value. But it names the small group who got most of the visible value. The conclusion notes that asking them directly is the cheap way to close the false-negative gap. A pointer only. Actually doing it is a new effort, and this study ruled surveys out.
