**Q3. What does the tiny conclusion hand the reader so "keep, don't invest" is actionable?**

The ticket flags for kill and keep are easy to write and hard to act on. You want the conclusion small, so it can't carry an implementation plan. Implementing the verdict is outside the study's scope anyway. The question is what minimal payload turns the verdict into a *decision* rather than a shrug.

1. **Explicit non-actions** — what "don't invest" rules out: no new tooling, no format redesign, no recurring metrics pipeline (already out of scope), and no kill.

The conclusion notes that asking them directly is the cheap way to close the false-negative gap. A pointer only—actually doing it is a new effort, and the study ruled surveys out.
